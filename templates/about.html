The One Place To Shop Your Tone.

A growing collection of everyday products made for your tone, gathered in one place. Huey is built around one idea: your skin tone shouldn't be something you re-explain to every brand you buy from. Build your profile once, and find what actually matches, not what's close enough.

Two steps.

1. Build your profile once

Upload a photo or take a selfie. We find your face, correct the colours for the lighting in the photo, and measure the colour of your skin. If you hold a sheet of white paper or a grey card next to your face, we use it to correct the brightness too. We then match that colour to the nearest tones on the Monk Skin Tone scale and suggest the closest one.

2. See your matches

Browse by category and see products actually suited to you, filtered to your tone instead of a generic, one-size-fits-most list. Huey matches and curates. It doesn't diagnose, and it doesn't manufacture. We're not a medical device or a clinical tool, and nothing here is a substitute for advice from a licensed dermatologist or physician.

Built on open standards, not guesswork

The Monk Skin Tone scale

Tone matching is built on the Monk Skin Tone Scale, a public, ten-point scale developed by Harvard sociologist Dr. Ellis Monk with Google, now used across Google Search, Photos, and Pixel's Real Tone work, and adopted by Medtronic to make clinical device testing more inclusive. Sensitivity guidance is informed by Fitzpatrick phototyping, the long-standing dermatological framework for sun/ingredient reactivity. We didn't invent a scale in a vacuum — we built on ones with real, public credibility. Learn more about the MST scale.

Your privacy

Your photo is never stored. For everything we do and don't keep, and how to delete it, read our privacy policy.

Limitations

Measuring skin tone from a photo is an estimate. Lighting, camera settings, beauty filters, makeup and even your screen can all shift the result, which is why you can always adjust it. For the most accurate result, hold a sheet of plain white paper or a grey card next to your face, use daylight, face the camera, and skip filters.

Huey isn't a medical tool. Your sun sensitivity answer is your own description, not a medical assessment, so please talk to a doctor or dermatologist about anything related to your skin's health.
